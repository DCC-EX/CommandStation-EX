## ST7798 SPI TFT Support Notes ##

Many TFT LCD displayes that use the SPI bus make use of the ST7789 display controller IC. They come in a range of resolutions up to 240 × 320 pixels. However, depending on the specific display size and form factor, the most common ST7789 resolutions include:

- 240 × 320, typical for 2.0" to 2.4" displays
- 240 × 240, typical for 1.3" and 1.54" displays
- 240 × 280, typical for 1.69" displays
- 170 × 320, typical for 1.9" bar-type displays
- 135 × 240, typical for 1.14" displays

All of these rely on the SPI serial bus, and being higher resolution are best suited to 32-bit microcontrollers with more RAM.

The new DCC-EX HAL driver for these displays makes use of the Adafruit ST7735/7789 TFT library, and the Adafruit GFX library to extend the DCC-EX Display capability that has until now handled just character LCD screens and OLED displays.

This capability is only presently supported on the ESP32 microcontroller, and not yet made as generic as it needs to be to be supported on a CSB1 or any of the STM32 range of microcontrollers.

By adding a simple HAL statement to myAutomation.h it creates a new display that can be the primary "LCD" for DCC-EX, or a secondary display.

The specification is as follows:

`HAL(ST7789, displayID, cs,dc,rst,width,height[,rotation[,textsize[,backlight[,active]]]]`

Where:

- displayID is 0 for the primary system display, or a higher number to act as a secondary
- cs,dc,rst are GPIO pins to which the chip select, data/command and reset pins of the ST7789 is attached. NB: the driver presently assumes GPIO 23 (MOSI), GPIO 19 (MISO), and GPIO 18 (SCK) of the ESP32 are to be used for the SPI bus. Todo: make this more generic!
- width and height are in pixels for the display being attached
- rotation is optional, with default being 0
- textsize is optional, with the default being 0 which optimises the text size based on resolution of the display
- backlight is optional, and is the GPIO pin used to drive the TFT backlight. NB: this is presently not dimmable, but could be with PWM support.
- active determines the polarity of the backlight signal, and is HIGH or LOW, with HIGH being the default.

Examples:

For an ST7789 170x320 display as the primary DCC-EX display, with the 370 pixels horizontally mounted as in the DIYMore 1.9" ESP32 module:

`HAL(ST7789,0,15,2,4,170,320,1,0,32,HIGH)`

For an OLED screen as primary, and a 170x320 ST7789 display as a secondary DCC-EX display, with the 370 pixels horizontally mounted as in the DIYMore 1.9" ESP32 module:

`HAL(HALDisplay<OLED>,0,0x3c,128,64)`
`HAL(ST7789,1,15,2,4,170,320,1,0,32,HIGH)`
